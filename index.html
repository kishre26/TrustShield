        💡 Online Safety Tips
        🎣 How to Spot a Phishing Email ▼
        • Check the sender's full email address — not just the display name.
        • Hover over links before clicking to see the actual destination URL.
        • Legitimate companies never ask for passwords via email.
        • Look for generic greetings like "Dear Customer" instead of your name.
        • Be suspicious of unexpected attachments, especially .exe, .zip, .docm files.
        • Urgency ("act within 24 hours!") is a classic psychological manipulation tactic.
        • Check for spelling errors and awkward grammar — often a sign of overseas scammers.
        • When in doubt, go to the company's website directly — never via the email link.
        📰 How to Identify Fake News ▼
        • Check the source: Is it a recognized, reputable publication?
        • Look for a byline and author credentials.
        • Verify claims on fact-checking sites: Snopes, FactCheck.org, PolitiFact.
        • Be skeptical of content with extreme emotional language or shocking headlines.
        • Check the publication date — old stories are often re-shared out of context.
        • Use reverse image search (Google Images / TinEye) to verify photos.
        • Real news articles cite sources — vague "experts say" is a red flag.
        • Satire sites (like The Onion) can be mistaken for real news.
        🔒 General Online Security Best Practices ▼
        • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on all important accounts.
        • Use a password manager and unique passwords for every site.
        • Keep your browser and operating system up to date.
        • Never reuse passwords across multiple sites.
        • Use a reputable antivirus/endpoint protection tool.
        • Be cautious on public Wi-Fi — use a VPN when possible.
        • Report phishing emails to your provider (Gmail: Report Phishing, Outlook: Report).
        • If you suspect identity theft, contact your bank and credit bureaus immediately.
        🆘 What to Do If You Were Phished ▼
        • Change passwords immediately — start with email, then banking.
        • Enable 2FA on all affected accounts right away.
        • Contact your bank if you shared any financial information.
        • Report the phishing attempt to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
        • Check your credit report for unauthorized activity (annualcreditreport.com).
        • Scan your device with a reputable antivirus if you downloaded anything.
        • Notify your IT department if it happened on a work device or account.
